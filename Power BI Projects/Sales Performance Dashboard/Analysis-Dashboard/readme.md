# 📊 Sales Performance Dashboard - Power BI

A Power BI dashboard built to analyze **sales, profit, returns, customer behavior, and product performance**.

The dashboard contains three interactive pages:

- **Overview**
- **Customer Insights**
- **Product Performance**

The goal of this project is to turn sales data into clear business insights that can help management understand **what happened, why it happened, what the risks are, and what actions should be taken.**

## 💡 Key Insights

### 1. Strong overall business performance

The business generated **$24.91M in sales** and **$10.46M in profit**, showing strong overall profitability.

### 2. Growth momentum has slowed

The year-level analysis shows a major change in growth:

**2021**
- Sales: **$9.37M**
- YoY Growth: **+45.58%**

**2022**
- Sales: **$9.19M**
- YoY Growth: **-1.49%**

This means the strong growth seen in 2021 was not sustained in 2022.

### 3. Returns need monitoring

Return performance also changed:

**2021**
- Return: **$276.35K**
- Return Growth: **+30.58%**
- Return Rate: **2.96%**

**2022**
- Return: **$277.30K**
- Return Growth: **+0.34%**
- Return Rate: **3.02%**

Sales declined slightly in 2022 while return value remained almost flat.

### 4. Monthly sales are not consistent

The monthly trend shows noticeable movement during the year, including a sharp decline around **June to July**, followed by recovery and stronger sales toward December.

### 5. Some products are major sales drivers

Several **Mountain-200** variants appear among the top-selling products, showing that some products contribute significantly to overall sales.

---

## 🎯 Recommendations

- Prioritize sustainable sales growth after the slowdown seen in 2022.
- Focus on high-performing products while reducing dependency on a small number of top-selling products.
- Maintain sufficient inventory for products that consistently drive high sales.
- Use monthly sales patterns to improve inventory, promotions, and sales planning.
- Give priority to products with high sales and relatively low return rates.
- Reduce return pressure by improving the performance of products with higher return rates.
- Protect the current strong profitability while pursuing further sales growth.

## 💡 Key Insights

### 1. Professional customers are the largest occupation-based revenue contributor

Revenue by occupation:

| Occupation | Revenue |
|------------|---------:|
| Professional | **$8.5M** |
| Skilled Manual | $5.4M |
| Management | $4.6M |
| Clerical | $4.0M |
| Manual | $2.5M |

Professional customers contribute the highest revenue among the occupation groups.

### 2. Lower-Middle Income customers are the biggest revenue contributor

Income group performance:

| Income Group | Sales | Profit |
|---------------|------:|------:|
| Lower-Middle | **$11.6M** | **$4.9M** |
| Low | $5.4M | $2.2M |
| Middle | $4.8M | $2.0M |
| Upper-Middle | $2.5M | $1.1M |
| High | $0.7M | $0.3M |

An important finding is that **high-income customers are not the biggest revenue contributors**.

The **Lower-Middle Income** group generates the highest sales and profit.

### 3. Customer value is not simply based on income

The data shows that higher income does not automatically mean higher sales contribution.

This suggests that customer buying behavior, product fit, and customer segment size are important factors.

---

## 🎯 Recommendations

- Prioritize **Lower-Middle Income and Professional customers** in targeted marketing campaigns.
- Focus on **customer retention and repeat purchases** among high-value customers.
- Use the Top 20 Customer analysis to support targeted customer retention strategies.
- Create targeted offers for the **High-Income segment** to increase its contribution.
- Align product offers and promotions with the buying behavior of the strongest customer segments.

## 💡 Key Insights

### 1. Strong product performance

The business sold more than **84K units** and generated **$24.15M in net sales**.

The overall **profit margin is 43.30%**, showing strong product profitability.

### 2. Returns are manageable overall

There were **1,828 returned units**, giving a total returned unit rate of:

**2.17%**

This means approximately **2.17 out of every 100 sold units were returned**.

The overall rate is manageable, but individual products may perform differently.

### 3. High sales does not always mean high profit

The product hierarchy shows that products can have different levels of:

- Sales
- Profit
- Profit Margin
- Returns

For example, the **Helmets** subcategory shows:

- Units Sold: **6,034**
- Revenue: **$205.83K**
- Profit: **$131.0K**
- Profit Margin: **65.71%**
- Returned Units: **188**

Its profit margin is significantly higher than the overall **43.30%**.

This shows why product performance should be evaluated using both sales and profitability, not sales volume alone.

---

## 🎯 Recommendations

A useful way to manage the product portfolio is:

| Product Performance | Recommended Action |
|---------------------|--------------------|
| High Sales + High Margin | **Scale and invest** |
| High Sales + Low Margin | **Optimize price/cost** |
| Low Sales + High Margin | **Growth opportunity** |
| Low Sales + Low Margin | **Review or consider reducing** |

Additional recommendations:

- Prioritize products with **high sales, strong margins, and low return rates**.
- Focus on products with **high sales and high return rates** to reduce return pressure.
- Maintain sufficient inventory for high-demand products.
- Increase focus on products with strong profit margins and healthy return rates.
- Avoid making inventory decisions based on **sales volume alone**.
